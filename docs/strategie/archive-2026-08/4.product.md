# Part IV — Products

Technology has value only when it becomes useful.

The Seshat Model defines how enterprise intelligence operates. The Seshat products make that model accessible to every employee.

The products split into two layers. Enterprise Knowledge, Intelligent Documents and Skills form the **platform layer** — the shared memory, document pipeline and reusable capabilities every agent draws on. Company Assistant and the growing family of Agent Workflows form the **agent layer** — what employees actually talk to and rely on to get work done.

Individually, each product solves a specific business problem. Together, they form a unified Enterprise Capability Platform.

# 9. Company Assistant

**Status:** 🟢 Most complete piece of the platform, per the builder's own assessment — not aspirational. `seshat-ui`'s Home screen (chat, quick actions, provider and model selector), session management, streaming responses, and multi-provider support already deliver most of what this chapter describes. The chat/session backend has real authorization test coverage (see the corrected testing gap note at the start of this section); the `seshat-ui` interface itself still has none — and the gap beyond that is Agent Workflows to delegate to, not the assistant itself.

---

## Purpose

The Company Assistant is the primary interface between employees and enterprise intelligence.

Rather than asking users to navigate software, search across multiple systems or understand complex business processes, the Company Assistant allows them to interact with their organization through natural language. It understands intent, answers directly whenever it can, and hands off to a dedicated Agent Workflow whenever a request needs one.

The assistant is not designed to replace people. It is designed to amplify their ability to work.

---

## Vision

For decades, enterprise software has required employees to adapt to applications. Every new system introduces another interface, another workflow, another way of working.

We believe this relationship should be reversed. Employees should interact naturally with their organization. The organization should adapt to them.

The Company Assistant represents this new interaction model. Conversation becomes the universal interface to enterprise capabilities — and, when a task is specialized enough to deserve its own agent, the entry point to it.

---

## The Problem

Modern employees spend a significant portion of their day navigating between applications: searching documents, reading emails, consulting procedures, switching between ERP systems, requesting information from colleagues, repeating the same questions.

The work itself is often simpler than finding the information required to perform it. Knowledge exists. Systems exist. Processes exist. Accessing them remains unnecessarily difficult.

---

## The Seshat Approach

The Company Assistant does not simply answer questions. It understands objectives.

Every conversation begins with intent. For direct questions, the assistant retrieves contextual knowledge from Enterprise Knowledge and answers immediately. For specialized, recurring jobs — managing an inbox, closing the books — it routes the request to the right Agent Workflow and stays available to coordinate the outcome.

The conversation becomes a single entry point into the entire enterprise, rather than a single application.

---

## How It Works

Every interaction follows the same conceptual lifecycle: understand the request, decide whether it can be answered directly or needs a dedicated agent, gather organizational knowledge, coordinate the necessary systems or Agent Workflows, and return a result the employee can act on.

The employee experiences a simple conversation. Behind the scenes, the enterprise collaborates with itself.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Intent understanding
- Conversational orchestration and routing
- Multi-agent coordination
- Context-aware conversation memory
- Direct knowledge retrieval for simple requests
- Delegation to specialized Agent Workflows for complex ones

---

## Typical Use Cases

- Answer company policy questions.
- Summarize projects and prepare meetings.
- Analyze operational performance.
- Investigate incidents.
- Hand off an invoice to the Accounting Agent.
- Ask the Inbox Agent for a status update on pending emails.
- Coordinate cross-functional work.

---

## Enterprise Benefits

- Faster access to knowledge.
- Reduced application switching.
- Better decision-making.
- Improved collaboration.
- Higher productivity.
- Consistent organizational knowledge.
- Lower onboarding time.

---

## Design Principles

The assistant always serves the organization rather than replacing it. It answers directly when it can, and delegates when a job deserves a dedicated agent — it does not try to be every product at once. Knowledge remains grounded. Human oversight remains possible. Every action respects enterprise governance.

---

## Future Evolution

Over time, the Company Assistant will evolve from a conversational interface into a true operational partner: proactively surfacing what needs attention, coordinating the growing family of Agent Workflows on the employee's behalf, and anticipating organizational needs while remaining aligned with enterprise governance.

---

> *The Company Assistant is not another chatbot. It is the conversational interface through which employees collaborate with the intelligence of their organization — and reach every specialized agent behind it.*

# 10. Enterprise Knowledge

**Status:** 🟢 Backend solid, 🟡 UI gap. Hybrid BM25 + vector search over ingested corpora is real, both locally and as an organization-scoped domain in the cloud control plane, and has been verified end-to-end with a real model. The builder's earlier "not ready" call was about the desktop/console experience specifically, not the retrieval pipeline — that's what's left to finish.

---

## Purpose

Enterprise Knowledge is the collective memory of the organization.

Its purpose is not merely to store information. Its purpose is to make organizational knowledge continuously available, contextual and actionable. Where Intelligent Documents turns individual files into structured data, Enterprise Knowledge connects that data — together with every other source — into one retrievable memory.

---

## Vision

Organizations do not suffer from a lack of information. They suffer from fragmented information.

Knowledge is distributed across documents, databases, applications, conversations and individual experience. Enterprise Knowledge connects these sources into a unified organizational memory.

Employees no longer search isolated repositories. They interact with organizational understanding.

---

## The Problem

Every enterprise accumulates knowledge over many years: projects, meetings, policies, technical documentation, customer relationships, operational procedures, lessons learned.

Unfortunately, this knowledge rarely exists in one place. Employees often spend more time searching for information than using it.

Knowledge becomes duplicated, outdated, forgotten, or lost when employees leave the organization.

---

## The Seshat Approach

Enterprise Knowledge treats every source of information as part of a single knowledge ecosystem.

Documents become connected. Emails become contextual. Business applications contribute operational knowledge. Conversations become searchable organizational memory.

Artificial Intelligence reasons across this unified context rather than isolated repositories. Knowledge is no longer passive. It actively supports enterprise execution.

---

## How It Works

Knowledge continuously flows into the platform from documents, business systems, databases, communication platforms, external services, enterprise policies and historical decisions.

Each source enriches the organizational memory. Artificial Intelligence retrieves only the context required for a specific objective.

Employees receive answers supported by enterprise knowledge rather than generic language models.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Unified enterprise search
- Knowledge graph
- Semantic retrieval
- Organizational memory
- Continuous knowledge enrichment
- Permission-aware access
- Source attribution
- Version awareness

---

## Typical Use Cases

- Find historical project decisions.
- Retrieve technical procedures.
- Explore customer history.
- Understand internal policies.
- Discover organizational expertise.
- Answer compliance questions.
- Support onboarding.
- Accelerate research.

---

## Enterprise Benefits

- Organizational memory persists.
- Expertise becomes reusable.
- Information becomes discoverable.
- Decision-making improves.
- Knowledge silos disappear.
- AI responses become trustworthy.

---

## Design Principles

Knowledge belongs to the organization. Every answer should remain grounded. Permissions must always be respected. Memory should improve continuously through everyday work. Knowledge is valuable only when it contributes to action.

---

## Future Evolution

Enterprise Knowledge will progressively evolve toward a living organizational memory capable of understanding relationships between people, projects, documents, decisions and enterprise capabilities.

---

## Deferred: Personal Research Workspace

A second usage pattern was considered alongside the org-wide search covered above: a personal, NotebookLM-style workspace where a user runs deep research or imports their own documents to build an ad hoc knowledge base, optionally blending in Enterprise Knowledge, then generates reports, slide decks or audio summaries from it.

Deliberately not being built now. It is a materially bigger surface (deep search, report/deck/podcast generation) than the org-wide search case, and the target customer's need right now is enterprise-facing, not a personal research tool — the use case is also inherently more private than organizational, which is a different trust model than what's being built first. Revisit once the org-wide search (this chapter) has real usage and the personal-workspace need is validated, not assumed.

---

> *Knowledge becomes valuable only when it is continuously available, trusted and connected to action.*

# 11. Intelligent Documents

Document reading and parsing (Docling or PaddleOCR, depending on configuration) already works and is tested — it's part of Company Assistant's read tools today, and enough for something like Inbox Agent to read an attachment without waiting on anything else. What's genuinely missing is importing and organizing data at enterprise scale, by department/team (HR, Legal, etc.) — that's the real gap this chapter describes, not the ability to read a file. Document Q&A and workflows built specifically around that departmental organization remain direction, not implementation.

---

## Purpose

Documents remain one of the primary ways organizations create, exchange and preserve knowledge.

Intelligent Documents transform static files into structured, active knowledge objects. Once processed, everything it extracts flows into Enterprise Knowledge, where it becomes part of the organization's searchable memory.

---

## Vision

Traditional documents are passive. Employees read them, interpret them, extract information manually, and repeat the same work.

We believe documents should understand their own content, participate in enterprise workflows, answer questions, trigger business processes, and maintain their own context throughout their lifecycle.

---

## The Problem

Organizations manage millions of documents: contracts, invoices, technical documentation, meeting notes, specifications, policies, reports.

Most of these documents remain isolated from enterprise intelligence. Information becomes trapped inside files. Employees manually extract, summarize and redistribute the same knowledge repeatedly.

---

## The Seshat Approach

Every document becomes an intelligent knowledge object. Artificial Intelligence understands its structure, its meaning, its relationships, its history, and its role within the organization.

Documents are no longer simply stored. They participate in enterprise capabilities — and feed the Agent Workflows that need them, such as the Accounting Agent processing an invoice.

---

## How It Works

When a document enters the platform, it becomes part of the enterprise knowledge ecosystem. Its content is analyzed, structured, linked to organizational knowledge, connected to workflows, and made available to enterprise reasoning.

The document continues evolving throughout its lifecycle while preserving its context and governance.

## Design Principles

Documents should participate in work rather than merely record it. Knowledge extracted from documents belongs to the organization. Automation should preserve human oversight. Every document should remain traceable and explainable.

---

## Purpose

Skills extend the intelligence of Seshat.

Each Skill represents a reusable enterprise capability that allows the platform to interact with business systems, perform specialized reasoning or execute domain-specific operations. Skills transform intelligence into practical action — and are the toolbox every Agent Workflow and the Company Assistant draw on, rather than each rebuilding the same integration.

---

## Vision

Organizations should not rebuild intelligence for every project. Capabilities should be reusable, composable, governed, and shared across the enterprise.

Skills provide this reusable layer. Once created, a capability becomes available throughout the platform.

---

## The Problem

Enterprise knowledge is often duplicated. Teams repeatedly implement similar automations. Business logic becomes fragmented. Integrations are recreated multiple times. Artificial Intelligence remains disconnected from operational expertise.

Organizations lose efficiency by rebuilding capabilities that already exist.

---

## The Seshat Approach

Every business capability becomes a reusable Skill: searching enterprise knowledge, generating reports, accessing ERP systems, querying databases, performing compliance checks, executing business rules.

Once developed, a Skill becomes available to every assistant, Agent Workflow and enterprise capability that needs it. Knowledge becomes reusable infrastructure.

---

## How It Works

Skills expose standardized capabilities. Artificial Intelligence selects the appropriate Skills according to business intent. Multiple Skills may collaborate within a single Agent Workflow.

Skills remain independent from the models that invoke them. This separation allows organizations to continuously evolve their intelligence without rewriting enterprise logic.

# 13. Agent Workflows

**Status:** 💭 Vision. Neither the Inbox Agent nor the Accounting Agent exist in code today — this chapter describes the pattern and the first two candidates, not a shipped product. What they will be built on (Enterprise Knowledge, Intelligent Documents, Skills, Scheduling) already exists.

---

## Purpose

Agent Workflows are specialized agents, each built to handle one recurring, well-defined business job end-to-end.

This is deliberately not a generic automation engine that customers configure themselves. It is a growing family of concrete agents, each built on top of Enterprise Knowledge, Intelligent Documents and Skills, each owning one real function of the business.

---

## Vision

Rather than offering an abstract workflow builder and asking organizations to design their own automation, Seshat ships direct, ready-to-work agents for the jobs organizations already do every day.

Each Agent Workflow is designed around one real business function, with the depth a generic orchestration engine cannot provide on its own.

---

## The Problem

General-purpose orchestration platforms promise flexibility. But flexibility without a validated use case is just a blank page — it shifts the design work back onto the customer instead of removing it.

What organizations actually need are dependable agents for the specific recurring functions they already run every day, built and refined against real use cases rather than assumed upfront.

---

## The Seshat Approach

Every Agent Workflow shares the same foundation: it draws on Enterprise Knowledge for context, Intelligent Documents for processing, and Skills for integration with existing systems. What differs from one Agent Workflow to the next is the job it owns.

The first two Agent Workflows are:

- **Inbox Agent** — manages communication across email, WhatsApp, Slack and other channels.
- **Accounting Agent** — manages the recurring work of accounting: invoices, reconciliation, reporting.

Further Agent Workflows will be added deliberately, one validated use case at a time, rather than built speculatively ahead of demand.

---

## 13.1 Inbox Agent

**Purpose.** The Inbox Agent manages the organization's communication load across every channel — email, WhatsApp, Slack, and other messaging platforms — as a single coordinated agent rather than a passive dashboard.

**Vision.** Modern organizations are overwhelmed by communication: emails, instant messaging, project management tools, approval systems, calendar invitations, notifications. We believe an inbox should not simply receive information. It should act on it.

**The Problem.** Employees spend an increasing amount of time sorting, prioritizing and responding to messages across disconnected channels. Important requests get buried. Approvals are forgotten. Critical incidents go unnoticed. Organizations lose productivity not because people fail to communicate, but because communication rarely becomes coordinated action.

**The Seshat Approach.** The Inbox Agent treats every message, on every channel, as a potential business event. It determines what requires attention, what can be handled automatically, what should be delegated, what can wait, and what must be escalated — and, when a message is really the start of another job, it hands it off. An invoice arriving by email can go straight to the Accounting Agent.

**How It Works.** Messages arrive from email, WhatsApp, Slack and other connected channels. The agent groups related activity, attaches relevant context from Enterprise Knowledge, drafts replies, highlights approvals, and routes anything outside its scope to the right person or the right Agent Workflow.

<!-- DIAGRAM: agent-example -->

**Key Capabilities**

- Multi-channel coverage (email, WhatsApp, Slack and more)
- Intelligent prioritization and triage
- Draft replies and suggested actions
- Cross-channel conversation summaries
- Delegation to other Agent Workflows
- Smart reminders and escalation

**Typical Use Cases**

- Triage and prioritize a shared inbox.
- Draft responses to routine requests.
- Summarize a long WhatsApp or Slack thread.
- Escalate a customer complaint.
- Forward an invoice email to the Accounting Agent.
- Track pending approvals across channels.

**Enterprise Benefits**

- Reduced notification overload.
- Faster response times.
- Fewer missed actions.
- One coordinated view instead of five open apps.

**Design Principles.** Communication should always lead toward action. Employees should receive context instead of noise. The agent should reduce interruptions rather than create new ones. Every action it takes must remain transparent and traceable.

**Future Evolution.** Channel coverage and autonomy will expand gradually, adding new channels and taking on more of the routine responses on its own as trust is established with real usage.

> *The Inbox Agent doesn't just organize messages. It decides what to do with them.*

---

## 13.2 Accounting Agent

**Purpose.** The Accounting Agent handles the recurring, rules-heavy work of accounting: receiving invoices, extracting and validating data, reconciling entries, and preparing reports for review.

**Vision.** Accounting is one of the clearest examples of work that is necessary, repetitive, and already governed by well-defined rules — exactly the kind of job an agent can own end-to-end, while every judgment call still goes through a human.

**The Problem.** Accounting teams spend a disproportionate share of their time on manual entry, chasing missing invoices, reconciling numbers across systems, and preparing the same reports every period. This work is essential but rarely where an accountant's expertise adds the most value.

**The Seshat Approach.** The Accounting Agent combines Intelligent Documents (to read invoices and receipts), Enterprise Knowledge (policies, chart of accounts, prior entries) and Skills (to connect to the organization's accounting or ERP system) into a single agent that owns the workflow from intake to a reviewable entry — never posting anything without human validation.

**How It Works.** An invoice arrives, often forwarded directly by the Inbox Agent. The Accounting Agent extracts and validates the data, checks it against policy and historical entries, prepares the coded entry or reconciliation, and routes it to a human for approval before anything is posted to the accounting system.

**Key Capabilities**

- Invoice and receipt extraction
- Reconciliation against existing records
- Classification against the chart of accounts
- Draft financial reports
- Compliance and policy checks
- Accounting/ERP system integration
- Human approval checkpoints and audit trail

**Typical Use Cases**

- Invoice intake and coding.
- Expense reconciliation.
- Monthly close support.
- Draft financial reports.
- Vendor payment preparation.
- Compliance checks on entries.

**Enterprise Benefits**

- Faster invoice-to-entry processing.
- Fewer manual entry errors.
- Faster month-end close.
- A full, auditable trail for every entry.
- More accountant time spent on judgment, not data entry.

**Design Principles.** Every entry stays reviewable before it is posted. Nothing reaches an external system without an audit trail. Accounting judgment stays with the accountant; the agent removes the mechanical work around it.

**Future Evolution.** Exact scope — which ERPs, which countries' compliance rules, which report formats — will be shaped by real customer engagements rather than assumed upfront. This chapter describes the pattern the agent follows; the details will be validated one deployment at a time.

> *The Accounting Agent doesn't replace the accountant. It removes the busywork standing between the accountant and the close.*

---

## Beyond These Two

Inbox and Accounting were chosen because they are recurring, well-understood, high-friction jobs found inside almost every organization. Every future Agent Workflow will follow the same discipline: start from a real customer need, not a hypothetical capability, and only add a new agent once that need has been validated.

---

> *Agent Workflows are not a platform for building automation. They are the agents that already know how to do the job.*

# 14. Scheduling

**Status:** 🟡 Implemented, untested. Cron-based scheduled jobs, with job history, are fully implemented — locally in `seshat-backend` (Scheduled Tasks, built-in TaskAgent) and as a multi-tenant automation domain in `seshat-server` (devices, jobs, runs, single-instance scheduler) — possibly needing minor adjustments, but not yet run through real testing. The blocker is hardware (the current dev machine needs an upgrade to test comfortably), not missing code.

---

## Purpose

Scheduling turns any capability of the platform — a knowledge query, a document check, eventually an Agent Workflow — into a recurring job that runs unattended, on a schedule, instead of waiting for someone to ask.

---

## Vision

Not every valuable piece of work starts with a question. Some of the most valuable work is the thing nobody remembers to trigger: the weekly reconciliation, the daily report, the recurring compliance check.

Scheduling makes those happen without anyone having to remember to ask.

---

## The Problem

Recurring operational tasks are exactly the kind of work that quietly falls through the cracks. Someone forgets to run the report. The weekly check gets skipped while its owner is on leave. The same request gets typed out manually every Monday morning.

None of this requires judgment. It requires reliability.

---

## The Seshat Approach

Any job the platform can do once, it can do on a schedule — a cron expression, an interval, or a specific run time. Each scheduled run executes with the same governance and permissions as if a person had asked for it directly, and its full history stays available: what ran, when, and what happened.

---

## How It Works

A job is defined once — what to run, on what schedule, against which organization's data and agents — and the scheduler takes it from there: queued, claimed, executed, and logged, with results available the same way a manual request's results would be.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Cron and interval-based scheduling
- One-off scheduled runs
- Job history and run status
- Governed execution (same permissions as a manual request)
- Audit trail per run

---

## Typical Use Cases

- Weekly cash reconciliation.
- Daily compliance or policy check.
- Recurring report generation.
- Scheduled knowledge-base refresh.
- Periodic inbox or communication triage.

---

## Enterprise Benefits

- Nothing recurring depends on someone remembering to trigger it.
- Consistent execution under the same governance every time.
- A full, auditable history of what ran and when.
- The foundation Agent Workflows will run on for anything recurring.

---

## Design Principles

Scheduled work follows the same governance as requested work — nothing runs with fewer checks just because a human didn't trigger it in the moment. Every run stays traceable after the fact.

---

## Future Evolution

Scheduling is the substrate the Agent Workflows will run on once they exist. An Accounting Agent should not need to be asked to reconcile the week — it should already be scheduled to.

---

> *The most valuable work is often the work nobody remembers to ask for. Scheduling makes sure it happens anyway.*

---

## Bringing Everything Together

Each Seshat product addresses a specific layer of enterprise intelligence.

Enterprise Knowledge, Intelligent Documents and Skills form the platform layer: the memory, the document pipeline and the reusable capabilities that every agent draws on. Company Assistant is the generalist entry point, answering directly whenever a request doesn't need a dedicated agent. Scheduling turns any of this into recurring, unattended work. Agent Workflows are the specialists built on top of all of it — the Inbox Agent and the Accounting Agent as first candidates, with more to come as real use cases are validated.

Private AI is named in this document set but not yet written as a full chapter — see the status table at the start of this section for what it already is (or isn't) today.

Individually, each product solves a focused problem. Together, they form a single Enterprise Capability Platform — a layer of shared infrastructure beneath a growing family of agents built for the jobs organizations actually need done.
