# Go-to-Market — Africa / Ghana

Working notes, not a finished chapter. Started from a real, concrete situation: a planned trip to Ghana to try to sell Seshat to businesses there — some formal, some informal, none of them pre-sold on "AI." To be deepened once more real conversations have happened; nothing here should be treated as decided.

Product source of truth is [4.product.md](4.product.md). This file is downstream of it, not the other way around — and so is the public website (`website/src/pages/services/`), once this is far enough along to update it.

## Starting complaint: the current website doesn't sell outcomes

`website/src/pages/services/ServicesGrid.tsx` and `ServicesHero.tsx` describe the three service wedges (document processing, AI knowledge team, back-office & finance) almost entirely in technical terms — RAG, OCR, hybrid retrieval, extraction pipelines.

A director evaluating this doesn't care whether it's RAG or something else. They care what it does *for them* — time saved, errors avoided, a problem that stops being a problem. The site currently reads like it's written for another engineer, not for the person who signs the check.

Fix comes later, after the product story (`4.product.md`) and the segment-specific pitches below are solid. Don't rewrite the site around guesses.

## Three concrete cases

Not chosen as a representative market sample — chosen because they're real, known, and structurally different from each other. Each stress-tests the pitch differently.

**Template used for each case, once decided (not while still a draft hypothesis):** Profile (who they actually are, concretely) → Base offer (what we propose, in outcome terms, tagged by what it costs to deliver) → Usage (how the business actually uses it day to day). Case 1 below is the first one worked through to this level; Case 2 and 3 are still at the draft-hypothesis stage until they get the same treatment.

### Case 1 — My father's business (informal trading, everything in his head) — DECIDED 2026-08-05

#### Profile

- Informal trading business: buys and sells through relationships and word-of-mouth, not contracts.
- Deals substantially through informal "agents" (intermediaries/middlemen) rather than direct, documented transactions.
- No formal bookkeeping: no invoices, no ledger, no CRM. Prices, debts, supplier terms, and customer history all live in his head.
- Runs mainly on WhatsApp/phone calls, not email or business software.
- If he's unavailable, the business's operational knowledge is effectively unavailable too — nothing is written down anywhere else.
- Price-sensitive, trust-based market — reputation and relationships matter more than any tool.

#### Base offer

Decided against building anything new for this profile specifically — the offer is entirely what's already real (Company Assistant) plus small, low-cost packaging on top. Nothing here waits on Documents, Knowledge, or any Agent Workflow.

**Ready today, zero new engineering (Company Assistant + its existing `web_search` tool):**
- **Price/market comparison** — "is this a fair price for X," "what's the going rate right now." Directly useful in a trading business, directly showcases value in one conversation.
- **Structuring sessions** — working conversations where the Assistant helps turn what's in his head into something written: suppliers and their terms, recurring customers and prices, current debts/credits. The deliverable is the resulting notes, not an app he has to operate alone.
- **Receipt / transaction-proof generation** — dictate a sale, get a formatted document back. Creates a paper trail that doesn't exist today, without needing Documents or OCR.
- **General personal assistant use** — drafting a message to a supplier, doing a quick calculation, thinking through a decision out loud.

**Small additional packaging (a Skill, plus Scheduling once it's actually tested):**
- **Debt/credit tracker** — who owes him, who he owes, due dates — with reminders once Scheduling is verified on the new hardware.
- **Informal supplier/customer directory** — a lightweight CRM built up through conversation over time, not entered into a form up front.

**Explicitly not offered yet:** anything implying automated WhatsApp triage (Inbox Agent), automated invoice reading (needs Documents), or a finished accounting system (Accounting Agent) — none of these exist in code. Promising them to the first, most relationship-critical customer is the fastest way to burn the referral chain this whole approach depends on.

#### Usage

A realistic day: before agreeing a purchase price with a supplier, he asks the Assistant what the going rate looks like. At the end of a day of sales, he dictates what happened and gets it turned into a simple written record — first time this business has any of that in writing. When a customer's payment is due, he gets a reminder instead of relying on memory. When he needs to message a supplier in a certain tone, he drafts it with the Assistant first. None of this asks him to change how he actually works — the Assistant sits alongside the same relationships and habits he already has.

#### Why this shape

This case is also the cheapest possible real-world test of the one solid piece of the platform (Company Assistant) before spending engineering time on Inbox/Accounting/Prospecting/RAG — and a working example here is what's supposed to generate the word-of-mouth referral into adjacent, slightly more formal businesses (see "Growth path hypothesis" below).

### Case 2 — Sachet water / beer manufacturer (Ghana, unnamed example) — DECIDED 2026-08-05

#### Profile (hypothesis, not a confirmed real company yet)

- Beverage manufacturer (sachet water or beer): production + distribution, inherently more formal than Case 1 by nature of the business (food/beverage manufacturing usually implies at least minimal registration/safety standards, even if still fairly artisanal).
- Already has invoices, delivery notes, some paper or Excel bookkeeping — but nothing connecting production, deliveries, and cash.
- Likely sells partly on credit to recurring retailers/kiosks — a common pattern in West African beverage distribution.
- Coordinates drivers and retailer orders mainly by phone/WhatsApp, same channel reality as Case 1.
- Buys inputs (packaging, sugar, bottles/sachets) from suppliers whose prices fluctuate.

#### Base offer

Same discipline as Case 1 — only what's real gets offered. This is *not* yet the "Documents + Accounting Agent" pitch described in `4.product.md` chapter 13, since neither exists in code.

**Ready today (Company Assistant + `web_search`):**
- Supplier price comparison for inputs (packaging, sugar, bottles).
- Structuring sessions: supplier terms, retail vs. wholesale pricing tiers, distributor network.
- Drafting purchase orders, distributor messages, retailer complaint responses.
- Ad hoc calculations: unit cost, margin, simple production planning.

**Light packaging (Skill + Scheduling once tested):**
- Retailer credit/debt tracking — likely more critical here than Case 1 given the volume of recurring credit customers.
- Restock/collection reminders.
- Supplier/distributor directory.

**Not yet deliverable — the real differentiator for this segment:** automatically reconciling delivery notes against invoices, which needs Accounting Agent itself (not built) as the orchestrating piece — the document-reading part it would rely on (extracting data from a delivery note/invoice) is already real, so this is lighter to build than it first looked. Don't promise it yet — but it's the strongest signal so far for prioritizing Accounting Agent in the build roadmap.

#### Usage

Before ordering packaging, he compares supplier prices through the Assistant. He dictates the day's deliveries, creating a clear written record of who owes what. He gets a reminder before collecting from a retailer who buys on credit. He drafts a purchase order or a distributor message with the Assistant instead of calling on the fly.

#### Why this shape

This is also the strongest case so far for a customer-facing Prospecting Agent (finding new retail outlets/distributors in a region) — unlike Case 1, where the owner doesn't have that kind of expansion need.

**Open question still unresolved:** no real companies identified in this space in Ghana yet — this is an archetype, not a prospect list.

### Case 3 — Medium/large enterprise, possibly already under an OpenAI/ChatGPT Enterprise contract — DECIDED 2026-08-05

#### Profile

- Formal organization with real IT/procurement process, unlike Cases 1 and 2 — buying decisions go through people, not just one owner.
- May already have an AI vendor relationship (ChatGPT Enterprise or similar) — the problem here isn't "convince them AI helps," it's "get in past an existing relationship."
- Distinct internal buying centers with different needs: IT/infrastructure, general staff productivity, and specific departments (HR, data/finance teams) — not one homogeneous buyer like Case 1 or 2.
- Realistic data governance/compliance concerns at this size, even if not always formalized.
- Has budget for real infrastructure, unlike Cases 1 and 2 — changes what's a credible ask.

#### Base offer — several distinct sub-offers, not one pitch

**A. Infrastructure pitch (to IT/compliance decision-makers) — the strongest, most differentiated offer, real today.** Their own servers, running Company Assistant for every employee, self-hosted — or the lighter version, Bring Your Own AI / hybrid: data stays on their infrastructure, strong external models used only where acceptable. Both deployment shapes are already real and already documented (`5.business-strategy.md`, "Deployment Models"). Don't compete on "better chatbot" — that's already lost if they have an OpenAI contract. Compete on data sovereignty, which sits on a different budget line (infrastructure/compliance) their existing SaaS contract can't answer.

**B. General staff productivity (to any employee, once A is approved) — mostly real today, two features close but not there yet.** Drafting, structuring, calculations (including real financial calculations, `internal/tools/math/financial`) — same base as Cases 1 and 2. Meeting summarization from a text transcript works today; from a raw audio recording, `pkg/audio`/whisper.cpp exists at the engine level but isn't wired into the product UI yet and hasn't been tested against paid models — describe as "coming," not "ready," to this more skeptical buyer. Same caveat for PPTX generation (Skill or MCP server, not yet integrated) and generated images.

**C. Department-specific surfaces (HR, data science, finance teams) — bespoke, not self-serve.** The underlying tools (bash, notebook read/edit) already exist. What's sold here is Seshat building a tailored surface + a curated set of Skills for that department, not a shelf product the client configures themselves — set that expectation up front so it isn't mistaken for a finished, self-serve offering.

**D. Not offered yet:** Inbox Agent (email tools) — 💭 vision, same as the other two profiles, even though this segment might value it most given email volume.

**E. "Add us, don't switch" — for prospects already committed to another AI vendor.** A company already paying for OpenAI (or another provider) doesn't need to be sold a replacement, which is a much harder sell than an addition. Bring Your Own AI (`5.business-strategy.md`) makes this a real, sellable angle: they keep their existing API relationship and keep paying that bill directly, Seshat sells the platform/orchestration layer on top of it. Removes the single biggest objection this segment has — switching cost and sunk investment in another vendor — by not asking for a switch at all. See `8.financial-model.md` for the cost-side implication: this also drops Seshat's own LLM cost exposure for that client to near zero, since their key covers usage.

#### Usage

IT approves and deploys self-hosted Company Assistant across the org (offer A). Employees use it daily for drafting, structuring notes, running calculations, and — once wired in — summarizing recorded meetings and generating slide decks (offer B, phased in as those features get integrated and tested). A department that signs on for more (HR, data science) gets a surface and Skills built specifically for them by Seshat (offer C), understanding that's a bespoke engagement, not an off-the-shelf module.

#### Why this shape

This segment is where the self-hosted/sovereignty pitch (offer A) matters most and the informal-market pitches (price comparison, structuring sessions) matter least — the inverse of Case 1. It's also the one profile where overselling B or C too early carries real reputational cost, since this buyer will actually test what's promised before signing, unlike a referral-based informal customer.

**Open questions:** Realistic to reach in Ghana specifically at this stage, or is this case more relevant elsewhere (regulated sectors, government, banks) later once there's a track record from Cases 1 and 2? What's the actual sales motion into an org that size without an existing local network?

## The dependency concern

Raised directly: "mon vrai problème c'est surtout rendre les entreprises dépendantes de moi." Self-hosted deployment + subcontracted setup/maintenance reframes this: the dependency isn't on a closed cloud vendor (the OpenAI-style lock-in), it's on *expertise* over infrastructure the customer actually owns. That's a healthier, easier-to-justify dependency to sell ("you keep your data, you pay for the skill") — but the pitch still needs to name this explicitly, not assume the customer infers it.

## Build roadmap beyond Company Assistant / Scheduling — decided 2026-08-05, corrected twice same day

Base for every customer, any profile: **Company Assistant** (includes document reading via Docling/PaddleOCR already) + **Scheduling** (once verified on new hardware). **Skills** descoped for now to viewing/reading (maybe editing) existing MCP-backed skills — no user-facing skill creation yet in this MVP.

**Second correction (same day):** the pattern found for audio/image/financial tools turned out to generalize further. `seshat`, `seshat-backend`, and `seshat-server` are consistently ahead of `seshat-ui` (desktop) and `seshat-console` — including for RAG/Knowledge (hybrid search, ingestion, embedder config: real, verified end-to-end against a real model at the backend) and document reading (Docling/PaddleOCR: real, already used by Company Assistant's read tools today). What actually never got built anywhere yet is the *enterprise-scale, department-organized* version of Documents (importing/structuring data by team — HR, Legal, etc.) — that's the genuine gap behind `4.product.md` chapter 11's vision, not the ability to read a file.

Practical effect: **Inbox Agent is not blocked on document handling.** It can already read attachments using the same tools Company Assistant uses today.

- **Inbox Agent** — the one genuine from-scratch build. No dependency on Documents or Knowledge. Likely first candidate by elimination.
- **Knowledge/RAG desktop+console UI** — not a new build, a finishing pass on an already-solid backend. Mainly unlocks Case 3 (large enterprise); Case 1 and Case 2 don't have a corpus worth querying yet.
- **Accounting Agent** — mainly serves Case 2 (manufacturer). Expected to be composition, not a from-scratch build: a specialized agent wired with complete Skills, potentially leveraging an existing open-source project (paperless-ngx) for the document-management side. Full value (automated invoice extraction) can already use the existing document-reading tools — no dependency on the enterprise-Documents gap either, only a lighter/manual-entry-first version needs to wait on anything at all.
- **Prospecting Agent** — dual purpose, both a sellable Agent Workflow for client sales/marketing teams *and* an internal tool for Seshat's own outbound effort into Ghana. The internal use case makes this higher-urgency than a pure future-product idea.
- **Documents, enterprise/department-organized sense** — the real remaining gap, not document reading itself. Mainly relevant to Case 3 (large enterprise); not needed for Cases 1 or 2.
- **Meeting summarization from audio, image generation, PPTX generation** — feature-level integration work that strengthens Case 3 and general productivity across all profiles: wire `pkg/audio` (whisper.cpp) and `pkg/image` into the product UI; add PPTX either via a Python Skill (inspired by `ppt-master`) or an existing MCP server (`Office-PowerPoint-MCP-Server`) rather than building a generator from scratch.
- **Department-specific surfaces (HR, data science, etc.)** — the underlying tools (bash, notebook read/edit) already exist; what's missing is a tailored frontend per surface (the "Apps page" idea in `future-ideas.md`) plus, per client, a bespoke set of Skills — not self-serve yet.

Per the "Beyond These Two" principle in `4.product.md`, this list isn't closed — new workflows get added only when a real, needs-driven case shows up (the market-comparison need surfaced this way for Case 1, and turned out to need no new build at all, just existing Company Assistant capability).

**Cross-cutting constraints, not per-feature:**
- **Hardware** — Scheduling is implemented but unverified; the current dev machine needs an upgrade before it can be tested properly.
- **Budget** — audio/image/financial tooling has only been exercised with free-tier/local models, never robustly tested against paid, production-grade models. Both constraints mean "works in the code" and "proven to work" are not the same claim anywhere in this roadmap — keep that gap explicit when pricing or promising anything to a real customer.
