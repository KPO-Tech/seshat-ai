Part I — Vision

1. Why Seshat Exists

The AI Revolution Has Started — But Enterprises Are Still Left Behind

Artificial Intelligence has reached a level where it can write code, analyze documents, answer questions, generate reports, summarize meetings and assist people in their daily work.

Yet, despite these extraordinary capabilities, most companies still struggle to integrate AI into their everyday operations.

The problem is no longer the intelligence of the models. The problem is everything around them.

Today's enterprises are filled with disconnected information, repetitive processes, fragmented software, and knowledge that exists only inside documents or in the minds of employees.

Employees constantly switch between dozens of applications:

  • Email
  • Messaging platforms
  • Shared drives
  • ERP systems
  • CRM systems
  • Internal documentation
  • Spreadsheets
  • Databases
  • APIs
  • Meetings

Every day they spend hours looking for information, copying data between systems, answering repetitive questions, validating documents and performing administrative tasks that add very little value.

Artificial Intelligence should solve these problems. Instead, today's AI tools often become one more application among many.

AI Is Not the Problem

Large Language Models have become incredibly capable. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Mistral, GLM, Kimi and many others can already reason, summarize, write, analyze and generate code.

Their limitation is no longer intelligence. Their limitation is context.

An AI cannot help a company if it does not understand:

  • the company's documents,
  • the company's business,
  • its internal procedures,
  • its employees,
  • its tools,
  • its permissions,
  • its workflows,
  • and the decisions that have already been made.

Knowledge without context is useless. Intelligence without action creates little value.

Companies Do Not Need Another Chatbot

Most AI products today focus on conversations. Another chatbot. Another prompt interface. Another assistant.

But enterprises are not looking for another chat window. They are looking for solutions that solve business problems.

They need systems capable of:

  • finding information instantly,
  • understanding company knowledge,
  • automating repetitive tasks,
  • coordinating specialized assistants,
  • integrating with existing software,
  • respecting security and permissions,
  • and acting safely inside business processes.

The value of AI is not measured by the quality of a conversation. It is measured by the time, money and effort it saves.

Enterprise Knowledge Is Broken

Every company owns an enormous amount of knowledge. Unfortunately, that knowledge is scattered everywhere.

             Company Knowledge

     PDF        Word        Excel

 Slack       Teams       Emails

 SharePoint  SQL       CRM

 ERP         APIs      Wikis

 Videos      Images    Audio

Finding the right information often takes longer than doing the work itself. Knowledge becomes obsolete. Employees leave. Expertise disappears. New employees require months before becoming productive.

This is not a documentation problem. It is a knowledge management problem.

Enterprise Work Is Fragmented

Knowledge alone is not enough. Companies also execute thousands of repetitive processes every day.

Invoices. Purchase requests. Customer support. HR approvals. Recruitment. Legal reviews. Sales follow-ups. Financial analysis.

Most of these processes require humans only because software cannot understand context. Artificial Intelligence changes this — but only if it can safely interact with company systems.

AI Must Become an Infrastructure

Electricity is no longer considered a feature. Internet is no longer considered a feature. Cloud computing is no longer considered a feature. Artificial Intelligence will follow the same path.

Within the next decade, AI will become part of the infrastructure of every organization. Every employee will work alongside AI. Every department will rely on specialized assistants. Every workflow will become partially autonomous.

The question is no longer whether companies will adopt AI. The question is how they will manage it.

Why Seshat Exists

Seshat exists to become the operating system that connects intelligence, knowledge, automation and enterprise software into a single coherent platform.

Instead of providing another chatbot, Seshat provides an AI operating system capable of:

  • understanding enterprise knowledge,
  • coordinating specialized AI employees,
  • automating business processes,
  • connecting existing software,
  • respecting security policies,
  • and remaining fully adaptable to every organization.

Its objective is simple: allow every employee to spend less time searching, copying, validating and repeating — and more time thinking, creating and making decisions.

Seshat is not built to replace people. It is built to augment them.

Vision Statement

We believe Artificial Intelligence should become an invisible layer of every organization, empowering people rather than replacing them.

Seshat exists to build that future.

Key Decisions

✅ Seshat is an Enterprise AI Operating System.

✅ AI is valuable only when connected to enterprise knowledge and workflows.

✅ Business problems always come before AI technology.

✅ Every capability must solve a measurable enterprise problem.

2. The Future We Believe In

Looking Beyond Today's AI

Technology has always evolved in cycles. Computers transformed how people create. The Internet transformed how people communicate. Cloud computing transformed how companies build software. Artificial Intelligence will transform how people work.

But AI is not simply another tool. It represents a new form of collaboration between humans and machines.

The future is not one where humans compete against AI. The future is one where humans and AI work together as complementary teammates.

Every Employee Will Work Alongside AI

Today, employees use software. Tomorrow, employees will collaborate with intelligent systems.

Every profession will gradually be augmented by specialized AI capable of understanding its domain, assisting daily work, and automating repetitive tasks.

An accountant will not open ten different applications. They will ask their AI to prepare financial reports, verify invoices, detect anomalies, and summarize accounting activities.

A recruiter will not manually screen hundreds of resumes. Their AI will identify relevant candidates, prepare interview summaries, and coordinate recruitment workflows.

A project manager will not spend hours writing meeting reports. Their AI will automatically summarize discussions, extract action items, assign responsibilities, and follow project progress.

The role of AI is not to replace expertise. Its role is to amplify it.

Every Company Will Have Its Own AI

Today, companies have:

  • email servers,
  • ERPs,
  • CRMs,
  • databases,
  • cloud infrastructure.

Tomorrow, every company will also have its own artificial intelligence. Not a public chatbot. Not a generic assistant.

But an AI that understands:

  • the company's knowledge,
  • its culture,
  • its vocabulary,
  • its processes,
  • its permissions,
  • its customers,
  • its products,
  • and its way of working.

This AI will become part of the organization's infrastructure, just like databases or cloud services are today.

Knowledge Will Become Living Knowledge

Today, knowledge is mostly static. It lives inside documents — PDFs, emails, slides, spreadsheets, wiki pages.

Unfortunately, static knowledge slowly dies. Documents become outdated. Procedures change. Employees leave. Expertise disappears.

Tomorrow, knowledge will become dynamic. Knowledge will answer questions. Knowledge will explain itself. Knowledge will recommend actions. Knowledge will evolve continuously. Knowledge will connect people, documents, conversations and decisions.

The company will no longer search for information. The information will find the company.

Companies Will Think in Capabilities, Not Applications

For decades, enterprise software has shaped the way organizations think.

When a company needs to improve communication, it buys a messaging platform. When it needs better knowledge management, it deploys a wiki. When it wants to automate finance, it purchases an ERP. The application becomes the solution.

But applications have never been the real objective. Organizations do not exist to use software. They exist to achieve outcomes: to communicate, to remember, to understand, to decide, to collaborate, to plan, to automate.

These are the true capabilities of an enterprise. Software is only one possible implementation.

Artificial Intelligence fundamentally changes this model. Instead of asking employees to learn dozens of different applications, organizations can now expose capabilities directly.

An employee should not have to wonder:

"Should I open Outlook, SharePoint or SAP?"

Instead, they should simply express an intention.

"Prepare tomorrow's client meeting."

The platform determines which enterprise capabilities are required.

  • Remember
  • Search
  • Understand
  • Analyze
  • Communicate
  • Plan
  • Decide
  • Act

Applications become implementation details. Capabilities become the foundation of the enterprise.

This shift — from application-centric thinking to capability-centric thinking — is one of the core ideas behind Seshat.

Software Will Become Invisible

Applications will not disappear. ERP systems will continue to exist. CRMs will continue to exist. Email platforms will continue to exist. Databases will continue to exist.

But users should no longer need to think about them. The software layer becomes an implementation detail. The capability layer becomes the interface.

Employees express intentions. The platform orchestrates capabilities. Capabilities orchestrate software.

Technology fades into the background. Business outcomes move to the foreground.

Automation Will Become Collaborative

Automation has traditionally relied on predefined rules: if this happens, do that. While useful, these systems struggle whenever context changes.

Artificial Intelligence introduces a new generation of automation. Instead of executing fixed instructions, intelligent systems can understand situations, reason about context, ask for clarification when necessary, and collaborate with humans.

Automation is no longer about replacing people. It becomes a partnership between human judgment and machine execution.

AI Will Become a Team, Not a Tool

Most AI products are designed around a single assistant. We believe this model will quickly reach its limits.

Companies are composed of specialists: finance specialists, HR specialists, legal specialists, support specialists, sales specialists.

AI should evolve in the same direction. Instead of one assistant doing everything, organizations will rely on specialized AI employees collaborating together.

Some will retrieve knowledge. Some will process documents. Some will monitor workflows. Some will generate reports. Some will interact with external systems. Some will coordinate the work of others.

Just like human teams, every AI will have its own expertise while contributing to a common objective.

Trust Will Become More Important Than Intelligence

As AI becomes increasingly capable, intelligence alone will no longer differentiate platforms. Trust will.

Organizations must know:

  • why an AI made a decision,
  • where information came from,
  • what actions were executed,
  • who approved them,
  • what data was accessed.

Explainability. Auditability. Security. Privacy. Permissions. These will become as important as reasoning itself.

The future belongs to AI systems that organizations can trust.

AI Should Adapt to Companies

Many software solutions force companies to adapt to predefined workflows. We believe the opposite.

Artificial Intelligence should learn how organizations operate, not force organizations to change.

Every company has its own vocabulary, culture, validation processes, internal rules and business constraints.

AI must adapt to these differences — not erase them.

The Enterprise Operating System

If AI becomes part of enterprise infrastructure... If knowledge becomes alive... If software becomes invisible... If specialized AI collaborate together...

Then organizations will require something entirely new. Not another application. Not another chatbot. Not another workflow builder.

They will require an operating system capable of coordinating intelligence across the entire organization — an operating system connecting people, knowledge, software, automation, memory, and decision-making.

This is the future we believe in.

The Role of Seshat

Seshat is our answer to this future. Not because companies need more AI, but because companies need a coherent way to organize it.

Our ambition is not to build the smartest model, nor the most powerful chatbot. Our ambition is to build the platform that allows every organization to safely adopt artificial intelligence as part of its daily operations.

Seshat exists to become the foundation upon which the next generation of intelligent enterprises will be built.

Vision Statement

We believe that every organization will one day operate alongside a trusted team of specialized artificial intelligences, seamlessly connected to its knowledge, software and people.

Seshat exists to build that future.

Key Decisions

✅ AI augments people instead of replacing them.

✅ Every company will own its own enterprise AI.

✅ Knowledge must become dynamic and actionable.

✅ Software should disappear behind user intent.

✅ Specialized AI are preferable to one general-purpose assistant.

✅ Trust is more valuable than raw intelligence.

✅ AI must adapt to companies — not the opposite.

4. How We See an Enterprise

Looking Beyond Organizations

Organizations are often described through their structure: Finance, Human Resources, Sales, Marketing, Operations, Legal, Information Technology. This perspective explains how people are organized.

Others describe organizations through their technology: ERP systems, CRM platforms, email, databases, document repositories, business applications, cloud infrastructure. This perspective explains where information is stored and how software is deployed.

Neither perspective explains how an enterprise truly operates. An enterprise is not simply a hierarchy of departments, nor is it a collection of applications. It is a living system where people, knowledge, technology and decisions continuously interact to achieve common objectives.

Understanding an enterprise therefore requires looking beyond its organizational chart and beyond its software landscape. It requires understanding what the organization is fundamentally capable of doing.

Enterprises Are Defined by Their Capabilities

Every organization, regardless of its industry or size, performs a set of essential activities. It communicates. It remembers. It understands information. It makes decisions. It coordinates work. It plans. It executes actions.

These abilities exist independently of any particular software solution. An organization may replace its ERP. It may migrate to a different CRM. It may adopt a new collaboration platform. It may modernize its infrastructure. Yet the enterprise must always retain the ability to communicate, remember, understand, plan and act.

These enduring abilities are commonly referred to as Business Capabilities within Enterprise Architecture. Unlike applications or organizational structures, capabilities remain relatively stable over time because they describe what an organization does, rather than how it currently does it.

Technology evolves. Business capabilities endure.

People, Processes and Technology

Every enterprise can be viewed as the interaction of four fundamental dimensions.

People

People create value. They make decisions. They collaborate. They innovate. Their knowledge and experience remain the organization's greatest asset.

Knowledge

Knowledge represents everything an organization knows: policies, procedures, documents, conversations, past decisions, customer information, operational experience. Without knowledge, organizations repeat mistakes instead of learning from them.

Processes

Processes coordinate work. They define how activities are performed, how decisions are validated and how value is delivered. Good processes improve consistency. Poor processes create unnecessary complexity.

Technology

Technology enables people and processes. It stores information, automates repetitive work, connects systems, and supports decision-making. Technology should empower organizations — not constrain them.

Together, these four dimensions form the foundation of every enterprise. Artificial Intelligence does not replace any of them. It strengthens the relationships between them.

Beyond Business Capabilities

Business Capabilities are already a well-established concept within Enterprise Architecture. Traditionally, they are used to describe and model how an organization operates.

We believe Artificial Intelligence allows these capabilities to become something more. Rather than existing only as architectural concepts, capabilities can become intelligent operational services.

An employee should not need to decide whether the answer is located in SharePoint, an ERP, a CRM or a database. They should simply express their objective.

The platform identifies the required capabilities. It retrieves the necessary knowledge, coordinates specialized AI, interacts with enterprise systems, executes business processes, and delivers the expected outcome.

Artificial Intelligence transforms business capabilities from static descriptions into active participants in the daily life of an organization. This perspective is one of the fundamental ideas behind Seshat.

A Capability-Oriented Enterprise

We believe the next generation of enterprises will no longer be organized primarily around applications. They will increasingly operate through intelligent capabilities.

Instead of navigating software, employees will express intentions. Instead of manually coordinating systems, intelligent platforms will orchestrate them.

Applications will continue to exist. Databases will continue to exist. ERP systems will continue to exist. But they will gradually become implementation details behind higher-level capabilities.

An employee will no longer think:

"Which application should I open?"

Instead, they will think:

"What am I trying to accomplish?"

The platform will determine how to achieve that objective. This represents a shift from application-centric work to capability-centric work.

Our Perspective

This philosophy influences every decision made within Seshat.

We do not see enterprises as collections of applications. We do not even see them primarily as collections of processes. We see them as collections of capabilities.

People perform these capabilities. Processes organize them. Technology supports them. Artificial Intelligence amplifies them.

Because capabilities remain stable while technology continuously evolves, they provide the strongest foundation upon which to build the enterprise systems of tomorrow.

Vision Statement

We believe the future enterprise will be defined not by the software it owns, but by the capabilities it can orchestrate through people, knowledge and artificial intelligence.

Key Decisions

✅ An enterprise is a living system, not a collection of software.

✅ Business Capabilities describe what an organization does — not how it is implemented.

✅ People remain at the center of every capability.

✅ Artificial Intelligence should amplify enterprise capabilities rather than replace them.

✅ Applications are implementation details; capabilities represent lasting business value.

5. The Problems Modern Enterprises Face

Organizations Have Never Had More Resources

Modern enterprises generate more information than at any other point in history: every email, every meeting, every contract, every customer interaction, every financial transaction, every project, every document, every discussion, every decision. All of this contributes to an ever-growing body of organizational knowledge.

At the same time, companies have invested heavily in digital transformation: ERP systems, CRM platforms, cloud infrastructure, Business Intelligence, knowledge bases, collaboration platforms, document management systems, automation tools, Artificial Intelligence.

On paper, organizations have never been better equipped. Yet many employees still struggle to accomplish simple everyday tasks.

The challenge is no longer creating information. The challenge is making it usable.

Information Is Everywhere — And Nowhere

Knowledge rarely exists in one place. It is distributed across hundreds of systems: documents, emails, chat conversations, databases, shared drives, enterprise applications, internal portals, cloud storage, and even inside people's memories.

Finding the right information often requires employees to know where to search before they can begin searching.

When information cannot be found quickly, work slows down. Employees interrupt colleagues. Questions are answered repeatedly. Existing work is recreated. Knowledge becomes fragmented.

Instead of accelerating organizations, information overload becomes a source of friction. Research consistently identifies fragmented enterprise knowledge as one of the primary barriers to productivity and effective AI adoption.

Software Has Become Fragmented

Digital transformation has brought tremendous value. It has also introduced unprecedented complexity.

Every department has adopted specialized software. Finance uses one platform. Human Resources another. Sales another. Legal another. Operations another. Each application solves a local problem. Few solve the enterprise problem.

Employees constantly switch between systems simply to complete a single task. What should be one workflow often becomes ten disconnected interactions.

The organization becomes increasingly dependent on software while simultaneously becoming more difficult to navigate. The problem is no longer the lack of tools. It is the lack of orchestration.

Knowledge Disappears Faster Than It Is Created

One of the most valuable assets of any organization is experience. Unfortunately, experience is rarely documented completely. Much of it exists only inside conversations, inside meetings, inside habits, inside individual employees.

When people change roles or leave the company, part of the organization's knowledge disappears with them. The enterprise continuously loses information it has already paid to acquire.

New employees spend weeks — or even months — rebuilding knowledge that once existed. The organization learns slowly because it forgets constantly.

Repetitive Work Consumes Human Potential

A surprising amount of enterprise work does not require human creativity: searching for documents, copying information between systems, preparing reports, summarizing meetings, answering recurring questions, validating routine requests, updating spreadsheets, following approval processes.

These activities are necessary. But they rarely create strategic value.

Highly skilled professionals often spend a significant portion of their day coordinating work rather than performing the work they were hired to do. Artificial Intelligence offers an opportunity to automate much of this operational friction.

Decision-Making Lacks Context

Good decisions require good context. Unfortunately, context is often fragmented.

Relevant information may exist in multiple systems. Historical decisions may not be documented. Important discussions may have occurred only in meetings or chat conversations.

As a result, employees frequently make decisions using incomplete information. This does not necessarily produce poor decisions. It produces inconsistent decisions.

Organizations become less predictable because knowledge is not shared consistently across teams.

AI Alone Does Not Solve These Problems

Large Language Models have dramatically improved the ability of computers to understand language. But intelligence alone does not eliminate organizational complexity.

An AI model cannot understand information it cannot access. It cannot respect permissions it does not know. It cannot execute workflows it is not connected to. It cannot preserve organizational memory by itself.

Without integration into the enterprise, AI simply becomes another application employees must learn to use.

The challenge is no longer intelligence. The challenge is orchestration. This explains why many organizations successfully deploy AI for individual productivity while struggling to generate measurable enterprise-wide business impact.

The Real Problem

Looking at these challenges individually can be misleading: fragmented knowledge, disconnected software, manual processes, lost expertise, information overload, poor collaboration, slow decision-making.

These are not independent problems. They are different symptoms of the same underlying issue.

Modern enterprises have become increasingly digital. But they have not become proportionally more connected.

Information exists. Knowledge exists. Processes exist. Artificial Intelligence exists. What is missing is a coherent way to orchestrate them together.

This is the problem Seshat is designed to solve.

Vision Statement

The greatest challenge facing modern enterprises is no longer the lack of information or technology. It is the inability to transform both into coordinated organizational intelligence.

Key Decisions

✅ Enterprise problems are systemic rather than isolated.

✅ Information overload is an orchestration problem, not a storage problem.

✅ Knowledge must remain accessible beyond individuals.

✅ AI should reduce operational friction, not introduce additional complexity.

✅ Orchestration creates more value than adding more software.

6. Why Today's AI Is Not Enough

Artificial Intelligence Has Never Been More Capable

Large Language Models have transformed what computers are capable of doing. They can write, analyze, summarize, reason, generate software, translate languages, understand documents, answer complex questions.

Only a few years ago, many of these tasks seemed impossible. Today, they have become commonplace.

Artificial Intelligence has reached an extraordinary level of capability. Yet despite these technological advances, many organizations still struggle to generate meaningful business value from AI.

The problem is no longer intelligence. The problem is everything surrounding it.

Intelligence Alone Does Not Create Enterprise Value

A language model can answer questions. An enterprise must make decisions.

A language model can summarize documents. An enterprise must coordinate hundreds of people.

A language model can generate code. An enterprise must operate reliably every day.

These are fundamentally different challenges. Organizations do not simply need intelligent models. They need intelligent systems — systems capable of understanding context, respecting governance, working alongside people, integrating with existing software, and executing business processes safely.

Enterprise value is not created by intelligence alone. It is created when intelligence becomes operational.

AI Without Context Is Incomplete

Every answer depends on context. Who is asking? What department are they part of? What information are they allowed to access? What decisions have already been made? Which documents are authoritative? What is the company's vocabulary? Which business rules apply?

Without this context, even the most advanced model can only provide generic intelligence.

Enterprise intelligence requires enterprise context. Context transforms information into understanding.

AI Without Integration Creates More Complexity

Many organizations introduce AI as a separate application. Employees open another browser tab. Another assistant. Another interface. Another workflow.

Instead of simplifying work, AI becomes one more tool that employees must learn to use.

The challenge is not adding AI. The challenge is embedding AI into the daily life of the organization.

Artificial Intelligence should work where people already work — inside their documents, inside their workflows, inside their communication tools, inside their business systems. Not outside them.

AI Without Memory Starts From Zero

Every interaction with a traditional AI assistant is largely independent. The conversation may remember a short history. The organization does not.

Companies accumulate knowledge over years: projects, meetings, decisions, lessons learned, customer interactions, policies, procedures.

This organizational memory should continuously enrich future work. An enterprise AI should learn from the organization — not start every conversation as if it had never existed before.

AI Without Orchestration Cannot Scale

Modern organizations rely on hundreds of interconnected activities: searching information, reviewing documents, approving requests, communicating with colleagues, updating systems, generating reports, monitoring operations.

No single model can efficiently manage every responsibility.

Enterprise AI requires orchestration — different capabilities, different specialists, different systems, working together toward a common objective.

The future is not a single intelligent assistant. It is a coordinated ecosystem of intelligence.

AI Without Trust Cannot Be Adopted

Organizations cannot rely on systems they do not understand.

Every recommendation should be explainable. Every action should be traceable. Every permission should be respected. Every decision should remain auditable.

Trust is not created by larger models. Trust is created by transparency, governance and human oversight.

Without trust, Artificial Intelligence remains an experiment rather than an enterprise capability. Research consistently identifies governance, trust, data quality and organizational readiness as the primary barriers to enterprise AI adoption rather than model performance itself.

AI Must Become Part of the Enterprise

Artificial Intelligence should not exist beside the enterprise. It should become part of it — part of its knowledge, part of its memory, part of its workflows, part of its governance, part of its decision-making.

Only then can AI move beyond isolated productivity gains and become an organizational capability.

Recent industry research shows that while AI adoption is widespread, most organizations have yet to embed it deeply into their operating model or redesign workflows around it, limiting enterprise-wide impact.

The Role of Seshat

This is where Seshat begins. Not by building a more intelligent model. Not by creating another chatbot. Not by replacing enterprise software.

Seshat exists to transform Artificial Intelligence into enterprise infrastructure. It connects knowledge, coordinates capabilities, orchestrates specialized AI, integrates existing systems, preserves organizational memory, supports governance, and enables organizations to safely adopt Artificial Intelligence as part of their daily operations.

Rather than asking:

"How can we use AI?"

Organizations can begin asking:

"How can AI become part of the way we operate?"

That is the question Seshat is designed to answer.

Vision Statement

We believe the future of Artificial Intelligence will not be defined by smarter models, but by the ability to transform intelligence into trusted, integrated and operational enterprise capabilities.

✅ Context is essential for enterprise intelligence.

✅ AI must integrate with existing systems rather than compete with them.

✅ Organizational memory is a strategic asset.

✅ Trust and governance are prerequisites for adoption.

✅ Orchestration creates enterprise value.

8. The Seshat Model

From Intent to Execution

Traditional software requires users to understand the organization. They must know which application to open, which documents to consult, which people to contact, which processes to follow.

Artificial Intelligence has simplified some of these interactions. Yet, in many cases, employees are still responsible for coordinating the organization themselves.

Seshat proposes a different approach. Instead of asking users to navigate the enterprise, the enterprise should understand the user's intent.

Every interaction begins with a simple objective — not with software, not with workflows, not with applications, but with intent.

The role of Seshat is to transform that intent into coordinated enterprise execution.

The Seshat Execution Model

Every request follows the same conceptual lifecycle: Business Intent → Intent Understanding → Capability Discovery → Enterprise Knowledge → Reasoning & Planning → Enterprise Orchestration → Execution → Observation & Learning, which feeds back into future intent.

Every request follows this lifecycle. Each stage is a responsibility, not a fixed technology — the model stays stable while what implements each stage evolves.

Each stage represents a responsibility rather than a specific technology. This allows the platform to evolve while preserving a stable conceptual model.

1. Intent Understanding

Everything begins with intention. Users should not need to describe systems. They should describe outcomes.

Examples include:

  • Prepare tomorrow's client meeting.
  • Analyze last month's sales performance.
  • Generate a compliance report.
  • Find every contract related to Project Atlas.
  • Onboard a new employee.
  • Investigate why production failed yesterday.

The objective is to understand what the organization is being asked to accomplish rather than how the task should be performed.

2. Capability Discovery

Once the intent has been understood, Seshat determines which enterprise capabilities are required.

For example, preparing a client meeting may require the ability to:

  • Remember previous interactions.
  • Retrieve relevant documents.
  • Analyze recent business activity.
  • Communicate with team members.
  • Generate summaries.
  • Plan upcoming actions.

Capabilities are independent from applications. They describe what the organization must do — not which software currently performs the work.

3. Enterprise Knowledge

Capabilities require context. Seshat gathers the knowledge necessary to accomplish the objective.

This knowledge may originate from:

  • Enterprise documents.
  • Databases.
  • Business applications.
  • Internal policies.
  • Emails.
  • Conversations.
  • Previous decisions.
  • Organizational memory.

Rather than searching isolated repositories, Seshat builds a unified understanding of the information relevant to the task. Knowledge becomes contextual rather than simply searchable.

4. Reasoning & Planning

Once sufficient context has been collected, Seshat determines how the objective should be achieved.

This stage may involve reasoning, decomposition, prioritization, validation, planning, risk assessment, and decision support.

The result is not merely an answer. It is an execution strategy.

5. Enterprise Orchestration

Execution often requires multiple participants: human experts, specialized AI systems, business applications, automation workflows, external services.

Seshat coordinates these participants as a single operational system. Rather than replacing enterprise software, it orchestrates their collaboration.

This orchestration is dynamic. The participants involved depend entirely on the requested objective.

6. Execution

Once the plan has been validated, actions are performed.

Depending on permissions and organizational policies, execution may include generating documents, updating enterprise systems, triggering workflows, communicating with stakeholders, producing reports, scheduling activities, and monitoring ongoing operations.

Execution always remains aligned with enterprise governance and human oversight.

7. Observation & Learning

Every execution produces new knowledge: new decisions, new documents, new outcomes, new experience.

Seshat continuously enriches the organization's collective memory. Future requests therefore benefit from previous work.

The enterprise becomes progressively more intelligent through experience rather than repetition. Learning belongs to the organization rather than to individual conversations.

A Model Independent of Technology

The Seshat Model is intentionally independent from implementation details. Future language models may evolve. Reasoning techniques may improve. New orchestration frameworks may emerge. Enterprise software will continue to change.

None of these changes alter the model itself. The model describes how organizational intelligence operates. Technology simply provides different implementations.

This separation allows Seshat to evolve continuously while preserving a stable conceptual foundation.

The Role of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence is an essential component of the model. It is not the model.

AI enables understanding, reasoning, planning, generation, automation. But enterprise intelligence also requires knowledge, governance, capabilities, orchestration, human collaboration, and organizational memory.

Only the combination of these elements transforms Artificial Intelligence into enterprise intelligence.

Vision Statement

Seshat transforms business intent into coordinated enterprise execution by combining capabilities, knowledge, orchestration and Artificial Intelligence within a single operational model.

Core Concepts

  • Intent before Tasks
  • Capabilities before Applications
  • Knowledge before Answers
  • Planning before Execution
  • Orchestration before Automation
  • Learning before Repetition

The Seshat Equation

Enterprise Intelligence is not produced by Artificial Intelligence alone. Instead:

Enterprise Intelligence =
Intent
+ Capabilities
+ Knowledge
+ Reasoning
+ Orchestration
+ Execution
+ Organizational Learning

Artificial Intelligence amplifies every stage of this process. It does not replace the process itself.

Closing Thought

Seshat is not defined by its agents. Nor by its language models. Nor by its integrations.

It is defined by the model through which organizations transform human intent into coordinated action. Everything else is an implementation.

13.1 Inbox Agent

Purpose. The Inbox Agent manages the organization's communication load across every channel — email, WhatsApp, Slack, and other messaging platforms — as a single coordinated agent rather than a passive dashboard.

Vision. Modern organizations are overwhelmed by communication: emails, instant messaging, project management tools, approval systems, calendar invitations, notifications. We believe an inbox should not simply receive information. It should act on it.

The Problem. Employees spend an increasing amount of time sorting, prioritizing and responding to messages across disconnected channels. Important requests get buried. Approvals are forgotten. Critical incidents go unnoticed. Organizations lose productivity not because people fail to communicate, but because communication rarely becomes coordinated action.

The Seshat Approach. The Inbox Agent treats every message, on every channel, as a potential business event. It determines what requires attention, what can be handled automatically, what should be delegated, what can wait, and what must be escalated — and, when a message is really the start of another job, it hands it off. An invoice arriving by email can go straight to the Accounting Agent.

How It Works. Messages arrive from email, WhatsApp, Slack and other connected channels. The agent groups related activity, attaches relevant context from Enterprise Knowledge, drafts replies, highlights approvals, and routes anything outside its scope to the right person or the right Agent Workflow.

Key Capabilities

  • Multi-channel coverage (email, WhatsApp, Slack and more)
  • Intelligent prioritization and triage
  • Draft replies and suggested actions
  • Cross-channel conversation summaries
  • Delegation to other Agent Workflows
  • Smart reminders and escalation

Typical Use Cases

  • Triage and prioritize a shared inbox.
  • Draft responses to routine requests.
  • Summarize a long WhatsApp or Slack thread.
  • Escalate a customer complaint.
  • Forward an invoice email to the Accounting Agent.
  • Track pending approvals across channels.

Enterprise Benefits

  • Reduced notification overload.
  • Faster response times.
  • Fewer missed actions.
  • One coordinated view instead of five open apps.

Future Evolution. Channel coverage and autonomy will expand gradually, adding new channels and taking on more of the routine responses on its own as trust is established with real usage.

The Inbox Agent doesn't just organize messages. It decides what to do with them.

13.2 Accounting Agent

Purpose. The Accounting Agent handles the recurring, rules-heavy work of accounting: receiving invoices, extracting and validating data, reconciling entries, and preparing reports for review.

Vision. Accounting is one of the clearest examples of work that is necessary, repetitive, and already governed by well-defined rules — exactly the kind of job an agent can own end-to-end, while every judgment call still goes through a human.

The Problem. Accounting teams spend a disproportionate share of their time on manual entry, chasing missing invoices, reconciling numbers across systems, and preparing the same reports every period. This work is essential but rarely where an accountant's expertise adds the most value.

The Seshat Approach. The Accounting Agent combines Intelligent Documents (to read invoices and receipts), Enterprise Knowledge (policies, chart of accounts, prior entries) and Skills (to connect to the organization's accounting or ERP system) into a single agent that owns the workflow from intake to a reviewable entry — never posting anything without human validation.

How It Works. An invoice arrives, often forwarded directly by the Inbox Agent. The Accounting Agent extracts and validates the data, checks it against policy and historical entries, prepares the coded entry or reconciliation, and routes it to a human for approval before anything is posted to the accounting system.

Key Capabilities

  • Invoice and receipt extraction
  • Reconciliation against existing records
  • Classification against the chart of accounts
  • Draft financial reports
  • Compliance and policy checks
  • Accounting/ERP system integration
  • Human approval checkpoints and audit trail

Typical Use Cases

  • Invoice intake and coding.
  • Expense reconciliation.
  • Monthly close support.
  • Draft financial reports.
  • Vendor payment preparation.
  • Compliance checks on entries.

  • Faster invoice-to-entry processing.
  • Fewer manual entry errors.
  • Faster month-end close.
  • A full, auditable trail for every entry.
  • More accountant time spent on judgment, not data entry.

Design Principles. Every entry stays reviewable before it is posted. Nothing reaches an external system without an audit trail. Accounting judgment stays with the accountant; the agent removes the mechanical work around it.

Future Evolution. Exact scope — which ERPs, which countries' compliance rules, which report formats — will be shaped by real customer engagements rather than assumed upfront. This chapter describes the pattern the agent follows; the details will be validated one deployment at a time.

The Accounting Agent doesn't replace the accountant. It removes the busywork standing between the accountant and the close.

Not every valuable piece of work starts with a question. Some of the most valuable work is the thing nobody remembers to trigger: the weekly reconciliation, the daily report, the recurring compliance check.

Scheduling makes those happen without anyone having to remember to ask.

The Problem

Recurring operational tasks are exactly the kind of work that quietly falls through the cracks. Someone forgets to run the report. The weekly check gets skipped while its owner is on leave. The same request gets typed out manually every Monday morning.

None of this requires judgment. It requires reliability.

The Seshat Approach

Any job the platform can do once, it can do on a schedule — a cron expression, an interval, or a specific run time. Each scheduled run executes with the same governance and permissions as if a person had asked for it directly, and its full history stays available: what ran, when, and what happened.

How It Works

A job is defined once — what to run, on what schedule, against which organization's data and agents — and the scheduler takes it from there: queued, claimed, executed, and logged, with results available the same way a manual request's results would be.

Key Capabilities

  • Cron and interval-based scheduling
  • One-off scheduled runs
  • Job history and run status
  • Governed execution (same permissions as a manual request)
  • Audit trail per run

Typical Use Cases

  • Weekly cash reconciliation.
  • Daily compliance or policy check.
  • Recurring report generation.
  • Scheduled knowledge-base refresh.
  • Periodic inbox or communication triage.

Enterprise Benefits

  • Nothing recurring depends on someone remembering to trigger it.
  • Consistent execution under the same governance every time.
  • A full, auditable history of what ran and when.
  • The foundation Agent Workflows will run on for anything recurring.

Design Principles

Scheduled work follows the same governance as requested work — nothing runs with fewer checks just because a human didn't trigger it in the moment. Every run stays traceable after the fact.

Future Evolution

Scheduling is the substrate the Agent Workflows will run on once they exist. An Accounting Agent should not need to be asked to reconcile the week — it should already be scheduled to.

The most valuable work is often the work nobody remembers to ask for. Scheduling makes sure it happens anyway.

Bringing Everything Together

Each Seshat product addresses a specific layer of enterprise intelligence.

Enterprise Knowledge, Intelligent Documents and Skills form the platform layer: the memory, the document pipeline and the reusable capabilities that every agent draws on. Company Assistant is the generalist entry point, answering directly whenever a request doesn't need a dedicated agent. Scheduling turns any of this into recurring, unattended work. Agent Workflows are the specialists built on top of all of it — the Inbox Agent and the Accounting Agent as first candidates, with more to come as real use cases are validated.

Private AI is named in this document set but not yet written as a full chapter — see the status table at the start of this section for what it already is (or isn't) today.

Individually, each product solves a focused problem. Together, they form a single Enterprise Capability Platform — a layer of shared infrastructure beneath a growing family of agents built for the jobs organizations actually need done.

What's In This MVP

Snapshot as of this document's writing — not a permanent state. See the status table in this chapter for sourcing.

Knowledge (RAG) — 🟢 Backend solid, 🟡 desktop/console interface in progress

Case 1 — My father's business (informal trading, everything in his head) — DECIDED 2026-08-05

Profile

  • Informal trading business: buys and sells through relationships and word-of-mouth, not contracts.
  • Deals substantially through informal "agents" (intermediaries/middlemen) rather than direct, documented transactions.
  • No formal bookkeeping: no invoices, no ledger, no CRM. Prices, debts, supplier terms, and customer history all live in his head.
  • Runs mainly on WhatsApp/phone calls, not email or business software.
  • If he's unavailable, the business's operational knowledge is effectively unavailable too — nothing is written down anywhere else.
  • Price-sensitive, trust-based market — reputation and relationships matter more than any tool.

Base offer

Decided against building anything new for this profile specifically — the offer is entirely what's already real (Company Assistant) plus small, low-cost packaging on top. Nothing here waits on Documents, Knowledge, or any Agent Workflow.

Ready today, zero new engineering (Company Assistant + its existing web_search tool):
- Price/market comparison — "is this a fair price for X," "what's the going rate right now." Directly useful in a trading business, directly showcases value in one conversation.
- Structuring sessions — working conversations where the Assistant helps turn what's in his head into something written: suppliers and their terms, recurring customers and prices, current debts/credits. The deliverable is the resulting notes, not an app he has to operate alone.
- Receipt / transaction-proof generation — dictate a sale, get a formatted document back. Creates a paper trail that doesn't exist today, without needing Documents or OCR.
- General personal assistant use — drafting a message to a supplier, doing a quick calculation, thinking through a decision out loud.

Small additional packaging (a Skill, plus Scheduling once it's actually tested):
- Debt/credit tracker — who owes him, who he owes, due dates — with reminders once Scheduling is verified on the new hardware.
- Informal supplier/customer directory — a lightweight CRM built up through conversation over time, not entered into a form up front.

Explicitly not offered yet: anything implying automated WhatsApp triage (Inbox Agent), automated invoice reading (needs Documents), or a finished accounting system (Accounting Agent) — none of these exist in code. Promising them to the first, most relationship-critical customer is the fastest way to burn the referral chain this whole approach depends on.

Usage

A realistic day: before agreeing a purchase price with a supplier, he asks the Assistant what the going rate looks like. At the end of a day of sales, he dictates what happened and gets it turned into a simple written record — first time this business has any of that in writing. When a customer's payment is due, he gets a reminder instead of relying on memory. When he needs to message a supplier in a certain tone, he drafts it with the Assistant first. 5. Risks and reserves — not to be forgotten

Usage risk: flat pricing against variable cost

Section 4's margin is calculated against an assumed usage level, not a measured one. If a client genuinely over-uses the agent under a flat subscription price, the LLM cost line grows while the price doesn't — the seller absorbs 100% of that risk under pure flat pricing. A sensitivity check: at the small-client price point (~$150/month, ~$63/month margin before salary allocation), a 2–3x usage surprise is absorbed comfortably; a 5x surprise (going from ≈$17 to ≈$84/month of LLM cost) eats nearly all of it.

Mitigation already available, not hypothetical: Quotas — per-user usage counters, daily/monthly — already exist as a real platform feature (README.md's feature list). This should be configured as a real guardrail per pricing tier from day one, not treated as optional. Practical options once real usage data exists: a capped/tiered plan (X messages included, overage billed), soft rate-limiting on heavy usage rather than a hard cutoff, or simply pricing with the sensitivity above already priced in as margin buffer.

Startup credits — an immediate opportunity, not a "later" one

American AI/cloud providers run active startup credit programs, most of them accessible now rather than after volume is proven:

Provider Credit Requirement
OpenAI up to $500K, no equity general startup program
Anthropic $100K (general) to $500K (via Y Combinator) pre-seed through Series A
Google Cloud up to $350K (AI-focused path) qualifying AI-first startup
AWS Activate $1K self-serve entry tier; up to $300K AI tier self-serve tier needs no backing; larger tiers need VC/accelerator backing

This directly addresses the budget constraint noted earlier (no robust testing against paid models so far) — applying is plausibly higher-leverage right now than waiting for volume to negotiate with anyone.

No equivalent found for Chinese providers. What DeepSeek, Z.ai, and MiniMax are doing instead is aggressive public price-cutting (DeepSeek cut cache-hit pricing up to 90% at one point) rather than startup-credit programs. Reaching out directly once real volume exists is a normal, worthwhile move — but there's no confirmed formal partner/credit program to point to the way there is for OpenAI/Anthropic/Google/AWS. Treat it as "worth trying," not "expected."

Enterprise due-diligence caveat for Chinese providers

Search results flagged this directly: enterprise buyers evaluating a China-model-based stack should weigh what isn't in the sticker price — enterprise support, formal SLAs, and Western compliance certifications are less mature at DeepSeek/Z.ai/MiniMax than at OpenAI/Anthropic/Google, and jurisdictional risk (data or requests touching a Chinese company's infrastructure) is a real objection a sophisticated Case 3 buyer may raise.

This is where the self-hosted pitch and the cost pitch reinforce each other. Several of these Chinese models ship as open weights (DeepSeek, GLM, MiniMax all have open releases) and can in principle be self-hosted entirely on the client's own infrastructure — which would neutralize the jurisdictional objection specifically (nothing calls out to a Chinese company's cloud at all) while keeping the cost advantage over comparable American models. Not yet verified: the actual hardware/GPU cost of self-hosting these specific model sizes at usable speed — needs its own costing pass before this becomes a real offer, not just a promising direction.

Selling to clients who already have another AI vendor

Raised directly: a company already using another provider's API (e.g., an existing OpenAI relationship) doesn't need to be sold a replacement — they can adopt Seshat as an add-on layer on top of the API access they already pay for. This isn't a new technical capability to build: it's the existing Bring Your Own AI model (5.business-strategy.md) applied as a sales angle specifically for Case 3 prospects who already have a vendor relationship. It removes the single biggest objection that segment has (switching cost, sunk investment in another vendor) by not asking them to switch at all — Seshat sells the platform/orchestration layer, the client keeps paying their existing API bill directly, and Seshat's own LLM cost exposure for that client drops to near zero since the client's key covers it.
