This page summarises the licence files of the repositories. The texts themselves prevail: read `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` in the repository, and ask a lawyer when a decision depends on them.
</Callout>

## The runtime: Apache-2.0

The Seshat runtime is published under the **Apache License 2.0**. The repository is [`seshat-runtime`](https://github.com/KPO-Tech/seshat-runtime), copyright 2025-2026 KPO-Tech. You can use it, change it, redistribute it and build a commercial product on it, as the licence says.

This covers the engine and everything you reach through the SDK (`pkg/`), the gRPC server and the other packages, with the exception described next.

## The terminal interface has another origin

The terminal interface, in `internal/seshattui`, was built from the terminal interface of [Crush](https://github.com/charmbracelet/crush) by Charmbracelet, Inc. Crush is published under the **Functional Source License 1.1, MIT Future License** (FSL-1.1-MIT):

- You may use, copy, modify and redistribute it for any purpose **except a Competing Use**. The licence defines a Competing Use as making the software available to others in a commercial product or service that substitutes for Crush, or for another product or service of Charmbracelet that uses it.
- Each version of Crush becomes available under the **MIT licence two years after it was published**.

What it means for the Seshat repository:

- The files of `internal/seshattui` that come from Crush stay subject to the FSL-1.1-MIT **in addition to** the Apache-2.0 terms of the rest of the repository. Apache-2.0 does not replace it for them.
- Which files are copied and which were written for Seshat is not recorded file by file. Until it is, the whole directory is treated as subject to the Crush licence.
- The engine does not use that code.

In practice:

| You want to | What it means |
|---|---|
| Run `seshat`, use the SDK or the gRPC server, in your company or your product | Nothing special. The Apache-2.0 terms apply to what you use |
| Redistribute the terminal interface inside a commercial coding-agent product | Read the Crush licence first, or leave `internal/seshattui` out and use the engine through the SDK or gRPC |

The authoritative statement is the `NOTICE` file of the repository, and `docs/licensing.md` explains it.

## SeshatOS and SeshatCloud

[SeshatOS](/en/products/seshatos) and [SeshatCloud](/en/products/seshatcloud) are **source-available**, not open source: the code can be read, and you can use and host it yourself, but the licence limits resale. Only the runtime is open source.

<Callout type="warning" title="The licences of these two products are drafts">
The `LICENSE` file of each repository says so itself: the SeshatOS licence (Apache-2.0 with a Commons Clause condition) and the SeshatCloud licence (modelled on the Functional Source License, with a small-deployment exception) have not been reviewed by a lawyer yet. Treat them as a statement of intent until they are final, and ask before you build a commercial offer on them.
</Callout>

The page [Open source and licenses](/en/open-source) compares the three on the website.
